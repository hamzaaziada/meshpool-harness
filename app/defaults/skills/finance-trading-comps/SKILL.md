# Trading comparables

## Peer set

- Start from how the target makes money (product, customers, business model, capital intensity, growth), not from
  its industry label. Aim for 5-10 peers; split into tiers (closest, broader) when the fit varies.
- Write one line per peer on why it is in the set. Exclude peers whose numbers are distorted (pending acquisition,
  restructuring) and say so.

## Numbers (same date for everyone; cite each)

- Equity value = diluted shares x share price (treasury method for options, if-converted for in-the-money converts).
- Enterprise value = equity value + total debt (incl. leases if the peers' EBITDA is after lease costs, consistently)
  + preferred + minority interest - cash and equivalents.
- Revenue, EBITDA, EPS: LTM from the latest filings; NTM and next fiscal year from consensus where public, else
  company guidance (say which). Calendarise to the same year end.
- Growth and margins next to the multiples, so a reader can see why a multiple is high or low.

## Output

- Table: company, market cap, EV, EV/Revenue (LTM, NTM), EV/EBITDA (LTM, NTM), P/E (NTM), revenue growth, EBITDA
  margin; then mean, median, 25th and 75th percentile rows. Highlight the target.
- Applied range: usually the interquartile range of the most relevant multiple, adjusted with a stated reason
  (growth premium, scale discount). Translate into an implied EV and equity value per share.
- For loss-making, high-growth targets EBITDA multiples may not apply; use EV/Revenue, EV/backlog or EV/capacity
  (e.g. per MW) and say why.

Never mix LTM and NTM in one column, and never compare EV multiples with equity-value metrics.
